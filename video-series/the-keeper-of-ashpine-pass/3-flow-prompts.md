# The Keeper of Ashpine Pass — Stage 3 (v3): Keyframes + Video Prompts

**8 clips × 8 s · 16:9 · Google Flow (Veo) · checked end-to-start**

> **Why v3:** v1 and v2 had to be corrected after each test clip, which cost credits. v3 changes the method: **every clip's first and last frame is made as a cheap still picture first.** Each clip's **end picture is the next clip's start picture**, so the clips must join. You only spend video credits once the pictures are right.

## PART A — How to work (saves credits)

### The method: keyframes
A **keyframe** is a still picture of one exact moment. There are 13 keyframes (K1–K13). Each video clip moves from one keyframe to the next:

| Clip | Start frame | End frame | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | K1 | K2 | Lodge camera (same in clips 1–4) |
| 2 | K2 | K3 | Lodge camera |
| 3 | K3 | K4 | Lodge camera |
| 4 | K4 | K5 | Lodge camera |
| 5 | K6 | K7 | Forest follow camera |
| 6 | K8 | K9 | Clearing camera |
| 7 | K10 | K11 | Boulder camera (same in clips 7–8) |
| 8 | K11 | K12 → K13 | Boulder camera |

*Clips 1→2→3→4 and 7→8 share keyframes, so they join perfectly. Between clips 4→5, 5→6 and 6→7, the camera moves to a new place. That's a normal film cut: what each character is doing still continues exactly.*

### Step by step
1. **Fix Walt's Character first.** Open Characters → Walt. If his portrait or body picture shows **any beard or stubble**, regenerate it: *"Same man, clean-shaven chin and upper lip, no beard, no stubble, keep the thick grey mutton-chop sideburns."* Flow trusts the picture more than the text, which is why the beard kept coming back.
2. **Make K1** with its full prompt (Part B) using **Nano Banana**, with the listed Characters attached.
3. **Make each next keyframe by EDITING the previous one:** upload the previous keyframe and paste the edit prompt. Editing keeps the room, the door and the faces identical.
4. **Check every keyframe** against its ✅ checklist. Fix the picture, which is cheap, before making any video.
5. Send me the keyframes if you want me to check them before you spend video credits.
6. **Make each video** (Part C): set the **start frame** and, if your Flow offers it, the **end frame**. Paste the video prompt. Use the **Fast or Quality** Veo model; Lite ignores more instructions.
7. If Flow can't take an end frame, use only the start frame. The prompt still describes the ending exactly.

### The lodge camera (repeated in K1–K5)
> **LODGE FRAME:** Static medium-wide shot from the customer side of the bar at chest height, 35mm lens, 16:9. A long dark wooden bar counter runs left to right across the middle of the frame; behind it are back shelves with bottles in shadow and an old brass cash register. At the LEFT edge of the frame: a stone fireplace with a crackling fire and mounted elk antlers above it. At the RIGHT edge of the frame: the log wall with the single heavy wooden front door (dark-stained vertical planks, one small square window, black iron latch), hinged on its right side and opening inward into the room. On the wall just left of the door: a black iron hook holding a coil of hemp rope, a folded brown rope net and a brown leather cap with ear flaps. Orange glass lamps hang from the beamed ceiling; frosted windows in the back wall show falling snow. Late afternoon, 1974. Warm amber firelight, cold blue light at the door window. Photorealistic, natural skin texture with visible pores, 1970s film look.

**Fixed positions in the lodge:** two lumberjacks always sit on stools at the LEFT part of the bar, backs to the camera. Walt is behind the counter in the CENTRE. Doreen is behind the counter at the RIGHT end. Nora (when present) stands on the customer side, right of centre, in profile facing left toward Walt, so her face and her right sleeve face the camera.
